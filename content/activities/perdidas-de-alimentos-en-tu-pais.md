# Pérdidas de Alimentos en tu País

## Resumen
En el mundo, cerca del 13 % de la comida producida se pierde entre la cosecha y la venta. Las pérdidas suelen ser mayores en los países en desarrollo, donde los productores tienen menos acceso a tecnología e infraestructura para proteger los cultivos y para guardar y transportar los alimentos. Las principales causas son enfermedades, plagas, malas técnicas de cosecha, manejo descuidado, mal embalaje y malas condiciones de transporte.

**Más mangos para todos:** las soluciones suelen ser simples. Los mangos maduros se echan a perder rápido por su humedad y su fragilidad. En Bangladesh, la FAO introdujo soluciones sostenibles y baratas: cajones plásticos para transportarlos, un tratamiento con agua caliente contra enfermedades y capacitación para cortar el tallo con tijera y guantes en vez de arrancarlo. Resultado: mangos de mejor calidad, que duran más, y una reducción del 70 al 80 % de los que se pudrían en cinco días.

## Desarrollo

### Parte 1: investigar
- ¿Cuánta comida se pierde por año en tu país?
- ¿Cuáles son las causas principales?
- ¿Qué pasa con la comida que se pierde?
- ¿Quienes trabajan en la cadena de suministro tienen información y capacitación para reducir pérdidas?
- ¿El gobierno tomó medidas?

### Parte 2: actuar
- Presenten lo que encontraron en un afiche, una presentación, una ficha o una infografía.
- Piensen qué políticas o cambios ayudarían. ¿Qué factores tendrían más impacto?
- Escriban al gobierno o al organismo ambiental nacional con sus hallazgos y recomendaciones.

> **Nota para el educador:** con los más chicos, alcanza con elegir un alimento cercano (por ejemplo, la fruta de una economía regional) y seguir su recorrido, con ayuda de un adulto para buscar la información.

## Cierre
- ¿En qué etapa se pierde más en tu país?
- ¿Qué recomendación les parece más importante?

D.2 FOOD LOSS IN YOUR COUNTRY (LEVEL 1, 2, 3)

AIM: Understand food loss in one's country and take steps to tackle it.
TIME NEEDED: 1-2 weeks to research, create a presentation and contact relevant authorities.
MATERIALS: Access to the Internet to conduct research. Younger participants may need adult help/supervision.

BACKGROUND
Globally, around 13 percent of food produced is lost between harvest and retail. Food loss tends to be a bigger issue in developing countries, where farmers may have less access to modern technologies and infrastructure to prevent crop damage during production and for food storage and transportation. The main causes of post-harvest losses are diseases, pest infestations, improper harvesting techniques, careless handling, poor packaging and transport conditions.
MORE MANGOES FOR ALL: The solutions to food loss are often simple. Take ripe mangoes, which are healthy and delicious. However, fresh mangoes spoil rapidly because of their high moisture content and delicate nature. FAO introduced sustainable, low-cost solutions to improve the quality and shelf-life of mangoes in Bangladesh. Using plastic crates for bulk packaging, for instance, reduced losses to a minimum during transportation, while a hot-water treatment to prevent disease expanded the fruit's shelf-life in shops and markets. FAO also trained farmers on harvesting tools and techniques, such as trimming the fruit stem with scissors and gloves rather than pulling them off by hand, to avoid injuring or staining the fruit. All these steps resulted in better quality mangoes, with longer shelf lives and an overall 70 – 80 percent reduction in the number of mangoes wasted due to decay over a period of five days.
Learn more at: www.fao.org/platform-food-loss-waste/food-loss/introduction/en

ACTIVITY
PART 1: Conduct research to understand where and how much food loss occurs in your country. When doing your research, consider the following questions:
* How much food is lost in your country each year?
* What are the main causes of food loss in your country?
* What happens to food that is lost?
* Do those working along the food supply chain have access to information and training on reducing food loss?
* Has your government taken steps to help reduce food loss?
PART 2:
* Collate your findings in the form of a poster, presentation, factsheet or infographic.
* Think about what policies or changes in your country could help address food loss. Based on your findings, what factors should you consider having the most impact?
* Write to your government or national environment agency, sharing your findings and including your recommendations for reducing food loss in your country.
