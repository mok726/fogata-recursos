# Aprendé a Leer las Etiquetas

## Resumen
Muchas veces tiramos comida en buen estado porque no entendemos las fechas de los envases. Durante una semana vas a revisar las etiquetas de lo que hay en tu casa, armar una lista por fecha y ayudar a que se consuma primero lo que vence antes.

## Para saber
Las etiquetas traen fechas distintas, y no todas significan lo mismo:
- **Consumir preferentemente antes de** (*best before*): tiene que ver con la **calidad** (frescura, sabor, color, aroma, nutrientes). Pasada la fecha, el alimento no necesariamente deja de ser seguro.
- **Fecha de vencimiento / Consumir antes de** (*use by*): tiene que ver con la **seguridad**. Pasada esa fecha, el producto no se debe consumir.
- **Vender antes de** (*sell by*): es para los comercios, para saber hasta cuándo exhibirlo. No quiere decir que después no se pueda comer.

> **Nota para el educador:** en Argentina, por ejemplo, las etiquetas suelen decir «Fecha de vencimiento» o «Consumir antes de» (equivalen al *use by*: después de esa fecha no se consume) y «Consumir preferentemente antes de» (equivale al *best before*: después puede perder calidad, pero no necesariamente es peligroso). Las fechas para comercios (*sell by*) casi no se ven en los envases. Revisá cómo se llaman en tu país.

## Desarrollo
1. Revisá las etiquetas de los alimentos de la heladera y la alacena. ¿Están claras? ¿Se entienden?
2. Hacé una lista ordenada por fecha de vencimiento y marcá lo que vence pronto:

| Alimento | Fecha de vencimiento |
|---|---|
| Cereal del desayuno | DD/MM/AAAA |
| Salsa de tomate | DD/MM/AAAA |
| Queso | DD/MM/AAAA |

3. Aplicá **PEPS** (primero en entrar, primero en salir): lo que vence antes se usa primero.
4. Pegá la lista en un lugar visible de la cocina y recordale a tu familia que usen primero lo que está por vencer cuando planifiquen las comidas.

**Consejos:**
- Las frutas, verduras y lo que se compra en ferias (aceitunas, quesos) no suelen traer fecha. Lo mejor es comerlos frescos: preguntale al productor cuánto duran y anotá cuándo los compraste.
- Si las frutas y verduras están arrugadas o muy blandas, probablemente se están pasando.
- Las verduras de hoja y los cítricos duran más en el cajón de la heladera o en una bolsa de red.
- Bananas, paltas y mangos van a temperatura ambiente; cuando maduran, se pueden pasar a la heladera.
- Cereales, pan y frutos secos duran más en frascos herméticos, mejor de vidrio.
- Los quesos duros duran unas 3 o 4 semanas en la heladera; los blandos, 1 o 2.
- Poné adelante lo que hay que usar primero.
- Pan con moho: se tira. Un huevo bueno se hunde en agua fría; uno malo flota.

## Cierre
Después de una semana:
- ¿Pudieron consumir cosas que estaban por vencer?
- ¿Entendés mejor qué significan las fechas?
- ¿La lista y el orden por fecha ayudaron?
- ¿Tiraron menos comida?

Revisen la lista cada dos o tres semanas.

**English version:**

A.2 LEARN ABOUT LABELS (LEVEL 1, 2, 3)

AIM: To reduce food waste due to correctly interpreting expiration dates.
TIME NEEDED: One week.
MATERIALS: * Access to your fridge and pantry/food storage area at home, to take stock of expiration dates. * Pen and paper or a phone/tablet to create the list.

BACKGROUND
Food labels include dates such as Sell By, Best By, Use By, and Best Before. All these dates can be confusing for consumers, especially because they do not represent a standardized system of food safety. Here is a bit of clarity:
* Best before: This date relates to the quality of the food - its freshness, taste, colour, aroma or nutrients. It does not mean that the food is no longer safe to eat after the date has passed.
* Use by: This date relates to the safety of the food. It signifies the date after which the product should not be consumed for safety and quality reasons.
* Sell-By: This label is aimed at retailers to inform them of the date by which the product should be sold or removed from shelf life. This does not mean the product is unsafe to consume after the date. Typically, one-third of a product's shelf-life remains after the sell-by date for the consumer to use at home (Adapted from the Institute for Food Safety and Health guidance). Learn more at: www.fao.org/3/ca6145en/CA6145EN.pdf

ACTIVITY
Examine the labels on the various food items in your home. Are they clearly labelled and easy to understand? Make a list of everything in order of their use-by dates and take note of the items that will expire soon. See the example below.
[ITEM NAME / USE BY DATE: Breakfast cereal, Pasta sauce, Cheddar cheese…]
Apply the FIFO approach in your home: first in, first out and make sure items get consumed before their use by dates.
Place your list in a prominent spot in your kitchen, to keep in mind when your family plans its meals. Remind your parents to first use the items that are expiring soon.

Tips to keep in mind:
Fresh produce such as fruit and vegetables, or items bought from farmers' markets, such as olives and cheeses, do not usually come with expiration dates. The general rule in such cases is to eat them at their freshest. Ask the producer how long the food should last, make a note of the date you purchased them and conduct some research on that item's shelf life. In general:
* If your fruit and veg are wrinkly or quite soft, they are probably expiring.
* Extend the shelf life of leafy greens and citrus by storing them in the crisper drawer or a mesh bag in the refrigerator.
* Tropical fruits, such as bananas, avocados and mangoes, should be stored at room temperature. They also will ripen better this way. Once they are ripe, you can move them to the fridge to prolong their lifespan.
* Store cereal, bread and fresh nuts in airtight – preferably glass – containers to keep them fresher longer.
* Hard cheeses last approximately 3-4 weeks in the fridge, while soft cheeses last approximately 1-2 weeks.
* Place the items to be used first at the front of your fridge or shelf so they are easy to locate.
Learn more at: www.fao.org/3/ca6145en/CA6145EN.pdf

QUICK TIPS: Mouldy bread? Toss it. A good egg sinks in cold water, a bad egg floats. Is your fruit looking mushy or wrinkly? Probably a sign to not eat it.

After one week
* Take stock of what you achieved over the week:
* Did you manage to consume several items that were about to pass their Use By dates?
* Do you have a better understanding of what date labels on food mean?
* Do activities such as making the list and storing items by date make it easier to consume food items before they expire?
* Did the activity help you reduce food waste at home?
Ongoing: Revise the list every two or three weeks to make sure you and your family consume food items at their freshest to reduce food waste as much as possible.
