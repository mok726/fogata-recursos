# Creativos con las Sobras

## Resumen
¡Las sobras no son aburridas! Muchas comidas del mundo nacieron justamente para aprovechar frutas, verduras o pan que iban a tirarse: guisos, el *gulash*, el *fattoush* o la *panzanella*. En la Argentina, por ejemplo, el budín de pan, las croquetas o el revuelto gramajo cumplen esa función. Cada uno prepara un plato rico usando principalmente sobras y después lo comparten en una comida a la canasta.

## Desarrollo
1. Revisá qué sobras hay en tu casa y pensá una forma creativa de convertirlas en un plato rico.
2. Preparalo en casa o con el grupo, si tienen una cocina grande.*
3. Armá una **tarjeta del plato**: valor nutricional aproximado, qué sobras usaste, la receta y cuánta comida salvaste de la basura.
4. Hagan una **comida a la canasta**: comen todos juntos y prueban los platos de los demás.

*Los más chicos tienen que cocinar acompañados y supervisados por un adulto.

## Cierre
- ¿Les gustó la actividad? ¿Qué platos les gustaron más?
- ¿Les sorprendió la cantidad de formas de aprovechar sobras?
- Copien las tarjetas para que todos se las lleven y prueben las recetas en casa.

> **Nota de seguridad (del original):** la comida tiene que ser siempre segura para comer. Si hay dudas (olor raro, textura extraña, moho), no se come. Lávense las manos antes de cocinar o manipular alimentos.

**English version:**

B.2 CREATIVE WITH LEFTOVERS: CHALLENGE (LEVEL 1, 2, 3)

AIM: To understand how reusing and repurposing leftovers can help reduce food loss and waste.
TIME NEEDED: 1 day to plan; 1 week to organize and hold the activity.
MATERIALS: Access to a kitchen, cooking utensils, and necessary ingredients for your dish. Either at school or at home.

BACKGROUND
Leftovers aren't lame! They can be reused for interesting new meals. Salvaging leftovers and giving them new life is a great way to reduce food waste in your home. Did you know that many dishes from around the world, like casseroles, goulash, fattoush and panzanella were invented with the goal of using up fruits, vegetables or bread and not letting them go to waste? Find tips for how to reuse leftovers in Section D.2. You can make the difference.

ACTIVITY
The idea is to prepare a delicious dish individually, mainly using leftovers. It may be more fun to cook together if your group has access to a large kitchen with multiple cooking stations. If not, you can bring the dishes you have prepared from home.
1. Take stock of leftovers in your house and come up with a creative way to turn them into a delicious dish.
2. Prepare your dish, either at home or with your group.*
3. Create an "information card" for your dish, detailing nutrition values, what leftovers were used, the recipe, and how much food was saved from going to waste.
4. Have a "pot-luck" party with your group, where everyone eats together and tries each other's recipes. *Younger participants must be accompanied and supervised by an adult for this activity.
5. Discuss the activity as a group.
* Did you enjoy the activity?
* Which dishes did you like the most? Were you surprised by how many ways leftovers can be used?
* Copy and share the food information cards with all participants so everyone can take them home and try the recipes out with their families.

SAFETY: Please remember that what is important is that all food should be safe to eat, should participants have any doubts, food should not be eaten as it could make you very sick. Learn more about food safety in YUNGA's Nutrition Challenge Badge.
