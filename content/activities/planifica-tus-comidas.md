# Planificá tus Comidas

## Resumen
Durante un mes vas a planificar las comidas de cada semana, junto con tu familia, para usar lo que ya hay en casa y comprar solo lo necesario. Al final, evalúan si tiraron menos comida que de costumbre.

## Para saber
Algunas formas de desperdiciar menos en casa:
- Planificar las comidas de la semana para usar todo lo que hay.
- Aplicar **PEPS** (primero en entrar, primero en salir; en inglés, FIFO): usar primero lo que vence antes.
- Comprar solo lo que se necesita y desconfiar de las ofertas «2 × 1», porque gran parte de lo que se compra así termina en la basura.
- Comprar frutas y verduras «feas», que están perfectas para comer.
- Pensarlo dos veces antes de tirar algo: ¿todavía se puede comer?, ¿se puede guardar?, ¿se puede transformar en otra comida?, ¿alguien lo querría?
- Hacer compost con los restos.

## Desarrollo
1. Elegí un mes en el que no tengas muchas comidas afuera ni invitaciones.
2. Al principio de cada semana, planificá cada comida con un adulto de tu casa: qué se puede cocinar y qué ingredientes ya tienen.
3. Usá una planilla como esta:

| Semana 1 del DD/MM | Lunes | Martes | Miércoles | Jueves | Viernes | Sábado | Domingo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desayuno | | | | | | | |
| Almuerzo | | | | | | | |
| Cena | | | | | | | |
| Colaciones | | | | | | | |

   Abajo, anotá las **compras necesarias para la semana**.
4. Tené en cuenta:
   - Planificar según lo que ya hay. Por ejemplo, si hay morrones en la heladera, pensá una comida que los use.
   - Al ir a comprar, comprar solo lo de la lista.
   - Buscar comidas sanas, nutritivas y sostenibles, con variedad de frutas y verduras.

## Cierre
Al final del mes, conversalo con tu familia y con tu educador:
- ¿Tiraron menos comida que de costumbre?
- ¿Cómo ayudó planificar cada comida?
- ¿Qué tan sanas y sostenibles fueron las comidas?
- ¿Qué fue lo más difícil? ¿Cómo se puede hacer más fácil para seguir haciéndolo? Por ejemplo: una lista en la heladera con lo que vence pronto, el plan semanal pegado en un lugar visible de la cocina, las recetas guardadas en el celular, o cocinar en familia con música.
- ¿Quieren seguir haciéndolo? ¿Y sumar a otros familiares o contarlo en redes?

A.1 MEAL PLANNING (LEVEL 1, 2, 3)

AIM: To reduce food waste at home.
TIME NEEDED: One month.
MATERIALS: Pen and paper or phone/tablet to track eating and food waste habits over one month.

BACKGROUND
There are lots of ways to reduce food waste at home. For example:
* Planning meals for the week in advance to make sure you use everything you have at home.
* Following the FIFO approach: first in, first out, so that you first use items that are due to expire sooner.
* Buying only what one needs.
* Avoid buying "2 for 1" offers because 80 percent of such goods usually end up getting wasted.
* Buying "ugly" looking fruits and vegetables at the store that no one wants but are perfectly fine to eat. If no one buys them, they will go to waste.
* Thinking twice before throwing any food item away. Is it still ok to eat? Can you save it for later? Can you repurpose it into something more interesting? Is there anyone around who would like to eat it? (Refer to Section D.2 You can make the difference for recipes and ideas on what to do with leftovers.)
* Creating compost bins so that food scraps are put to good use.

ACTIVITY
This activity involves planning your meals carefully for one month, so choose a month that works for you when you have not been invited out to eat or other such commitments. At the beginning of the week, carefully plan each meal. You may need to work with a parent to discuss what would be feasible to make and which ingredients you already have at home. Use the template below, or something similar, to plan the week's meals and the necessary groceries.

[Template: WEEK 1 OF DD/MM – Monday to Sunday – Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snacks – Groceries needed for the week]

Points to keep in mind:
* Plan meals according to what you already have at home, to use those items up first. For example, if you have some peppers in the fridge, then plan a meal that uses them up.
* When shopping for your week's meals, only buy what's on the list. Avoid straying from the plan.
* Always try to plan healthy, nutritious and sustainable meals, and eat a variety of fruit and vegetables!

At the end of the month, discuss the activity with your family, teacher and leader.
* Did you waste less food than usual this month?
* How did planning each meal of the week help?
* How healthy and sustainable were your meals?
* Is this practice something you would like to continue doing each week? How about making a commitment to wider family members and inviting them to join you? Or how about sharing your efforts on social media with your followers?
What was hard about it? How can you make meal planning easier to make sure you keep doing it? By for example:
* Keeping a checklist of items in your fridge or freezer that will expire shortly and need to be consumed.
* Placing a weekly meal plan at a prominent place in the kitchen.
* Print out or save to your phone/tablet any recipes you will need for the week.
* Making cooking a family activity, where everyone can contribute and spend time together. Perhaps you could put on your favourite music while you're at it.
