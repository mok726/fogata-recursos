# Semana sin Desperdicio

## Resumen
¿Sabías que por año se desperdicia al menos el 17 % de toda la comida producida en el mundo, entre comercios y hogares? Son unos 931 millones de toneladas, con las que se podría alimentar a millones de personas que pasan hambre. Durante una semana vas a anotar toda la comida que se tira en tu casa. La semana siguiente, el grupo se desafía a ver quién logra reducirla más.

## Desarrollo

### Semana 1: el diario
Anotá todo lo que se tira en casa en un **diario de prevención del desperdicio**:

| Comida | Tipo de alimento | Cantidad | Qué se hizo con eso | Motivo | Costo* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desayuno | Avena | Medio tazón | A la basura | Estaba lleno | $… |
| Almuerzo | | | | | |
| Cena | | | | | |

*Preguntale a un adulto de tu casa cuánto costó, más o menos, lo que se tiró.

Las partes no comestibles (cáscaras de huevo, carozos, semillas) **no** cuentan como desperdicio. Sí cuenta lo que se podía comer y se tiró: sobras, comida vencida o que se echó a perder.

Al final de la semana, fijate qué se tiró más y por qué. Pensá cómo reducirlo:
- ¿Preferís otra cosa de desayuno?
- ¿Las porciones son muy grandes?
- ¿Se podrían aprovechar mejor las sobras?
- ¿Ayudaría planificar mejor las comidas?
- ¿Sirven unos papelitos en la heladera para recordar lo que hay que comer antes de que se eche a perder?

### Semana 2: el desafío
Repetí el diario, pero ahora es un desafío del grupo: ¿quién reduce más su desperdicio con lo que aprendió? Anotá las pequeñas acciones diarias que te ayudan.

## Cierre
Al final de la semana, junten los diarios y comparen la semana 1 con la 2:
- ¿Redujeron mucho el desperdicio?
- ¿Qué pasos les sirvieron? ¿Qué no funcionó?
- ¿Les va a ayudar a desperdiciar menos de ahora en adelante?

Festejen a quien más lo redujo, como el grupo decida. Conversen también sobre qué hacer con lo que igual se tira: compost en casa o el contenedor de orgánicos.

> **Nota de seguridad (del original):** la comida tiene que ser siempre segura para comer. Si hay dudas (olor raro, textura extraña, moho), no se come. Lávense las manos antes de cocinar o manipular alimentos.

> **Nota para el educador:** como la comida en casa la deciden los adultos, conviene valorar también el esfuerzo y las ideas, no solo quién «ganó».

## Texto original completo de la actividad

**English version:**

B.1 NO WASTE WEEK: CHALLENGE (LEVEL 1, 2, 3) [The heading in the original booklet reads "B.1 MEAL PLANNING", an apparent typo; the activity is "No waste week: challenge".]

AIM: To become mindful of daily food waste and take steps to reduce it.
TIME NEEDED: One week.
MATERIALS: Pen and paper or phone/tablet to track eating and food waste habits over two weeks.

BACKGROUND
Did you know that at least 17 percent of all food produced worldwide is wasted yearly, from retail stores to households? That is 931 million tonnes of food, which could feed millions of people who are going hungry each day.
Learn more at: www.fao.org/3/cb6601en/cb6601en.pdf, www.un.org/en/observances/end-food-waste-day/resources, stopfoodwaste.org

ACTIVITY
For one week, keep track of all food waste at home using the suggested template below.
Note: Inedible parts of food products (e.g. eggshells, fruit and vegetable pips and stones, etc.) are not considered food waste, so there is no need to include them in the diary. However, food that could have been eaten but was thrown away is waste, such as leftovers, food that is past its "use by" or "best before" date, or food that has gone off or become mouldy.
Food waste prevention diary – WEEK 1 OF DD/MM – DAY 1: Meal / Food type / Amount / How disposed / Reason / Cost* (e.g. Breakfast – Oatmeal – Half a bowl – In the rubbish bin – Felt too full to eat – 1 euro)
(Adapted from: www.fao.org/3/cb6601en/cb6601en.pdf)
* Ask your parents about the estimated cost of each food item that went to waste.

After one week
Identify what food was wasted the most and why. Think about how to reduce that waste. E.g.:
* Is there something else you would prefer to eat for breakfast?
* Are your portion sizes too big?
* Could you be using leftovers more efficiently?
* Would planning your meals more carefully help?
* Can you place sticky notes on the fridge and elsewhere to remind family members about food items that need to be eaten before they spoil?

The challenge
Repeat the activity, but this time, make it a challenge for the group to see who can minimize their food waste based on their findings from the previous week. Make a note of small everyday actions that help you be more mindful about reducing food waste.
At the end of the week, join with your group and discuss how it went.
* Were you able to reduce food waste significantly compared to the previous week?
* What steps did you take to reduce food waste?
* What worked and what didn't?
* Will the activity help you become more mindful about minimizing your food waste in the future?
As a group, compare everyone's food waste prevention diaries of Week 1 and Week 2 to assess who minimized their waste the most. Celebrate the winner(s) with a prize, party, schoolwide announcement, or any other way your group agrees.
Have a discussion about the wasted food and how can it be best disposed of, for example by doing home composting or by throwing it in the organic recycling bin.

SAFETY: Please remember that what is important that all food should be safe to eat, should participants have any doubts food should not be eaten as it could make you very sick. Learn more about food safety in YUNGA's Nutrition Challenge Badge.
